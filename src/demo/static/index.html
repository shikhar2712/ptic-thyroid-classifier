    About

    This research tool describes whether one H&E image of thyroid tissue looks like papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). It learned from 1,006 clear-cut patches of 24 patients, scanned at one hospital on one scanner (Leica GT450, 40×). Not a medical device and not for diagnosis: it describes how a patch looks, not a patient's diagnosis, and it has not been validated on other hospitals, scanners or stains. Such images are refused on purpose.

    Images from the PTIC dataset are scored only by the models that never saw that patient, so their answers are fair. Any other image is scored by the whole ensemble (E3). A PTIC image is recognised only as the original, unchanged file: a cropped, re-saved or screenshot copy is scored by the whole ensemble, most of which learned from that patient, so its answer can look better than it should.

    Heat maps

    A heat map (Grad-CAM) colours each part of an image by how much it pushed the model towards PTC: blue means none, yellow to red means strong. The map is smoothed for display and uses one colour scale for every image. It is made only for answered images. A heat map shows where, not why, and it cannot rule out the main worry: if the model had learned the colour of PTIC's stain or scanner, the map would still “look at the cells”, because that colour is everywhere in the tissue.
